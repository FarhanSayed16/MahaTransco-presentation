# **Presentation Roadmap** 

## **Presentation Roadmap** 

### **The one sentence this whole session must prove** 

_"AI can already do a meaningful part of an officer's paperwork, translation, and analysis workload today — for free, in minutes, if you know how to ask."_ 

### **Audience psychology (why the structure is what it is)** 

Senior IAS Group A officers are busy, skeptical of hype, and have sat through bad tech presentations before. They will disengage the moment it feels like a sales pitch or a college seminar. They respond to: - Being shown a real result before being told any theory. - Governance-relevant examples (circulars, disaster response, RTI, grievance data) — not generic marketing copy. - Straight talk about risk: what can go wrong, what must never be uploaded, how to verify. - Being asked to participate (show of hands, guesses), not lectured at. 

### **The narrative arc (90 minutes)** 

|**Phase**|**Time**|**Goal**|**Mode**|
|---|---|---|---|
|1. The Hook|0–5 min|Break the assumption that AI output is obviously fake|Show, don't tell|
|2. Reframe|5–10 min|"This is not about ChatGPT. This is about a digital assistant for governance."|Talk|
|3. Landscape|10–15 min|Different AI exists for different jobs — text, image, video, docs, code|Talk + visual|
|4. Live Demos|15–55 min|Text → Image → Video → Document Intelligence, each demoed live with pre-loaded prompts|Demo-first|
|5. Concepts|55–70 min|Prompt engineering, tokens, hallucination, verification — explained now that they've seen the power|Talk|
|6. Data Privacy|70–78 min|What never to upload; enterprise/on-prem alternatives|Talk (serious tone)|
|7. Deepfakes|78–85 min|Real risk to governance and reputation; detection habits; new IT Rules 2026|Talk (serious tone)|
|8. Close + Q&A|85–90 min|"AI will not replace officers. Officers who use AI will replace officers who don't."|Talk + open floor|



### **Design principles** 

**Demo first, theory later.** Every concept is introduced only after the audience has watched it happen. 

- **One idea per slide.** If a slide needs a sub-bullet to explain the bullet above it, split it into two slides. 

- **No jargon before it's earned.** Don't say "token" or "context window" until Phase 5, and even then, translate it into a paperwork analogy immediately. 

- **Governance examples only.** Every demo prompt is something a District Magistrate's office, not a startup, would actually need. 

- **Assume the internet will fail.** Every live demo has a screenshot backup ready to switch to instantly. 

### **What "done" looks like for this workshop** 

An officer leaves the room able to answer, for themselves: which AI tool do I open for which job, what must I never type into it, and how do I tell if something online is fake.
