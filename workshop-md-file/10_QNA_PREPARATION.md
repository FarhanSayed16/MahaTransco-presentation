## **10 — Q&A Preparation** 

Likely questions from senior IAS Group A officers, with strong, honest answers. Better to under-promise and be precise than to oversell. 

**Q: Is this AI-generated content admissible/usable in an official government document as-is?** A: No — treat every AI output as a first draft. It needs the same review, fact-check, and sign-off any draft would get before it becomes an official document. The value is in the time saved getting to a first draft, not in skipping review. 

**Q: What happens to the data we type into these tools?** A: On free/consumer tiers, it may be used to improve the model unless you're on an enterprise plan that guarantees otherwise. This is exactly why we never put confidential or citizen personal data into a free tool — full detail in the data privacy handout. 

**Q: Is there a government-approved version of these tools we should be using instead?** A: This is an evolving area under the IndiaAI Mission's governance framework. For anything beyond routine, non-sensitive drafting, the safe default is an enterprise-tier subscription with a no-training guarantee, or an on-premise/local deployment for genuinely sensitive data — check with your IT/NIC cell for what's currently approved in your department. 

**Q: Can this replace our translation unit / graphic design cell / IT cell?** A: No. It removes the _first draft_ delay — minutes instead of hours or days — but a human translator, designer, or developer is still needed for anything going out officially, especially anything legal, technical, or citizen-facing at scale. 

**Q: How do we know the AI isn't just making things up?** A: You don't, by default — that's the hallucination risk. The mitigation is: never treat an AI-stated fact, number, date, or citation as verified until you've checked it against a real source. Tools like NotebookLM help because they cite the specific page they're drawing from, which makes verification faster. 

**Q: What about the officers/staff who don't have great English or tech skills — will this leave them behind?** A: The opposite, if introduced well — translation and plain-language reformatting are some of the strongest use cases shown today. A junior officer with a good prompt can now produce a formal-English draft they couldn't easily write themselves. The skill that matters is asking clearly, not typing fast or knowing English perfectly. 

**Q: Isn't this just hype that will be outdated in six months?** A: The specific tools will keep changing — that's true. But the underlying skill (knowing which type of AI tool to reach for, how to prompt it clearly, and how to verify its output) will remain useful regardless of which specific product wins out. That's why this session teaches the pattern, not just today's app names. 

**Q: What's the actual cost to the department?** A: Everything demoed today has a usable free tier. Costs only arise if a department scales up to enterprise/paid tiers for data-privacy guarantees, or commissions an on-premise deployment — both legitimate, deliberate procurement decisions, not something to enter into casually. 

**Q: Who is accountable if an AI-assisted document contains an error?** A: The same officer who would have been accountable if they'd drafted it themselves without AI. AI doesn't change the accountability chain — it's a drafting aid, not a decision-maker or a signatory. 

**Q: Can AI analyze our own department's data (grievances, scheme disbursement, dashboards)?** A: Yes, and this is one of the highest-value applications — see the analytics section in 05_GOVERNMENT_USE_CASES.md . The caveat is the same as for text: analytical suggestions from AI are hypotheses to verify, not conclusions to act on directly, especially for anything driving policy or enforcement action. 

**Q: What if someone in my office already used a public AI tool with sensitive data — what do we do now?** A: Flag it to your IT/data protection point of contact immediately rather than treating it as a one-off mistake to ignore — this session exists precisely so it doesn't happen again, and early flagging matters more than assigning blame. 

**Q: Is this you trying to sell us something?** A: No — every tool discussed today has a free tier, this isn't procurement, and I have no commercial stake in any of them. The goal is purely to make sure you're not missing tools that could save real time on real paperwork.
