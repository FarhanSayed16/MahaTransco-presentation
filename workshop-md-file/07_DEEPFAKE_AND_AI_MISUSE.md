# **07 — Deepfakes and AI Misuse** 

Serious tone, continues directly from the data privacy section. 

## **Why this matters specifically to this audience** 

Senior officers are disproportionately likely to be _targets_ of deepfakes (fake statements attributed to them) and disproportionately likely to be the _point of verification_ when a fake circulates in their jurisdiction (a fake FIR, a fake evacuation order, a fake video "showing" an incident). Both roles require the same skill: fast, calm verification. 

## **What is technically possible today** 

- **Voice cloning:** A short (sometimes under a minute) real audio sample is enough to generate convincing fake speech in that voice, including fabricated official announcements or instructions. 

- **Face/video synthesis:** Realistic fake video of a real, named person saying or doing something they never did. 

- **Fabricated documents/evidence:** AI-generated or AI-altered images presented as photographic evidence (fake FIRs, fake damage photos, fake crowd/protest images). 

- **Fake social proof:** AI-generated "eyewitness" images or fabricated crowd scenes to falsely support a claim (e.g., "a fake morcha/protest photo"). 

## **India's regulatory response — what changed in 2026** 

- **IT Rules Amendment 2026** (notified 10 Feb 2026, effective 20 Feb 2026) formally brought **"Synthetically Generated Information" (SGI)** — a technology-neutral term covering deepfakes and other AI-altered audio/visual/audio-visual content — under the Intermediary Guidelines & Digital Media Ethics Code Rules, 2021. 

- **Mandatory, persistent labelling:** Platforms must ensure SGI content is clearly and prominently labelled (visual label for visual content, audio disclosure for audio content) for its **entire duration** — not just a one-time watermark — and platforms cannot allow that label to be removed or altered. 

- **Provenance/metadata:** Where technically feasible, permanent metadata or unique identifiers must be embedded to trace the origin of the content. 

- **Faster takedown for the worst content:** For non-consensual or morphed sexual/intimate imagery (including deepfake pornography), the takedown window was cut from 24 hours to **2 hours** . 

- **Safe harbour clarification:** Platforms that remove SGI in good-faith compliance (even via automated tools) are explicitly protected — this was designed to encourage faster platform action. 

- **Context:** This built on the broader **India AI Governance Guidelines** unveiled in November 2025 under the IndiaAI Mission — a "Do No Harm," human-centric framework preferring to work through existing law (like this IT Rules amendment) rather than a single omnibus AI law. 

_(Note for you as presenter: this is a fast-moving regulatory area — if presenting after mid-2026, do a quick check for any further amendments before the session, since implementation details and challenges to these rules are actively being litigated/discussed.)_ 

## **Your verification checklist (this is the practical takeaway for the audience)** 

1. **Pause before forwarding anything that demands urgency.** Urgency ("forward immediately," "share before it's taken down") is a manipulation pattern, not a reason to skip verification. 

2. **Check the source, not just the content.** Who sent it first? Is it from an official, verifiable account/channel? 

3. **Reverse-image-search unusual visuals** before trusting or sharing them. 

4. **Verify through an independent official channel** — call the office directly, check the official website/press release — never verify by replying to or re-forwarding the same message. 

5. **Look for the mandated label.** Under the 2026 rules, legitimate platforms must now visibly label AI-generated content — absence of a label isn't proof of authenticity, but presence of tampering signs (unnatural blinking, mismatched lighting, audio-lip sync issues, inconsistent shadows) is a red flag worth a second look. 

6. **Escalate through the proper channel** , not by publicly reacting first — especially for anything implicating law and order or a named official. 

## **What NOT to do in this section of your talk** 

Do not name a real, currently-serving public figure as an example of "here's a deepfake of them" — use a generic, clearly fictional example 

- ("imagine a fake voice note attributed to a District Magistrate") instead. 

- Do not attempt to demonstrate voice-cloning or face-swap tools live — this crosses from "awareness" into "here's how to do it," which is the wrong lesson for this audience. 

Do not overstate detection reliability — no detection method is foolproof; the checklist above is about building a habit of verification, not a guaranteed test. 

## **Closing line for this section** 

_"The same demos you saw an hour ago — that convincing rainfall notice, that realistic disaster photo — are exactly the tools someone with bad intent could use against your office. The defense isn't avoiding AI. It's the same verification discipline you already apply to any unverified field report, applied consistently to anything digital as well."_
